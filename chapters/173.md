Chapter 173 - PMC

\# The World of Stardust!

It's already widely known that the latter part of this world's original comic [Stardust!] flows in an extremely devastating direction for Stardust.

But in truth, it's not just devastating for Stardust alone—the original world setting itself becomes increasingly bleak as the story progresses.

Ability users are gradually increasing worldwide. Particularly troubling is that most of them end up becoming villains, making the situation increasingly difficult for every country.

Especially in America and Europe, well, they've always been a mess with villains far stronger than those in Korea, so that goes without saying. Though they do have equally strong heroes to maintain a precarious balance of power, even those regions are finding it increasingly difficult. Especially after the fall of the hero who was considered America's last bastion.

And this chaos was no different in Korea and other countries.

Take Japan, for instance—it was absolute mayhem with yakuza controlling half the country and confronting both the government and the Association. Though arguably, the government was more corrupt than the yakuza, but either way, the situation was dire. Even with Korea's mess, at least rebels hadn't taken over half the country.

Of course, in the latter part of the original story, the yakuza are betrayed, split apart, and picked off one by one by the government... but since the government was already corrupt, the chaos actually worsens. You could call it the rise of competing warlords.

And then there's China. China is even more ridiculous. They have a major villain organization called the Fire Dragon, which consumes half of that massive country in the final arc. Some heroes even join their side, which says it all.

Just hearing about it sounds like a complete disaster.

And Korea, holding its place among the three East Asian nations, naturally shows no less chaos than its neighbors.

The only heroic forces are: Stardust working alone, Icicle who's busy just managing Yu-seong Corporation, and Shadow Walker who turned dark after his girlfriend died. That's the entirety of their strength. With so many villains packed into such a small country, public safety begins to collapse. And then the Moonlight Cult opens a portal to another dimension, bringing monsters into the mix, making everything even worse.

In other words, things are relatively peaceful now only because I'm covering things, but the country will continue to fall into greater chaos.

Especially as the proportion of ability users increases, the number of villains will continue to grow. Already, even just in Seoul, terrorist incidents have increased compared to before. And that's despite me not carrying out any terrorism lately.

And if this chaotic situation continues, Stardust will naturally be torn apart. What's happening now is just child's play—later it will be hell. No matter how strong Stardust is, she only has one body and can't work non-stop without rest. Especially after the Moonlight Cult incident, when public safety is on the verge of collapse.

To prevent that hell from breaking loose, I'm planning to establish a PMC.

Why don't ability users want to become heroes? Because B-rank and below get paid poorly while receiving tremendous criticism for even small mistakes. If even well-paid A-rank users don't want to be heroes, why would others?

That's how we end up with surplus manpower—people with abilities who don't work as heroes. And some of them fall into villainy once their livelihoods become difficult or they find the right opportunity.

After thinking about this for a long time, I came to a conclusion.

Yes. I'll just spend a ton of money and hire them myself. They don't want to be heroes because they can't make money and only get criticized? Then let them join a PMC where they can make good money without criticism. Don't let those abilities go to waste.

It's a win-win business that prevents potential villains while nurturing quasi-heroes.

That's the PMC business I've planned.

The business concept itself is simple. Just privately hire ability users, train them according to my preferences, and build up a fighting force. That's it. And later, they'll be useful when Korea falls into chaos.

But. There's a bit of a problem.

No. Not just a bit—there are many problems.

Even if I have the funding, how would I gather people, and what about the government and Association raising hell about collecting ability users to form a rebel army?

And that's where Lee Seol-ah's role comes in.

Lee Seol-ah, who currently controls all other corporations, the government, and the assembly. If her Yu-seong Corporation is doing the hiring, no one can stop her. She's already seized control of most media outlets, so who could possibly block her?

Successfully persuading Lee Seol-ah to join the PMC business, we've been preparing diligently, and quite some time has passed.

"Phew... Da-in. It's all done. Now there won't be anyone in political circles or elsewhere raising objections. The same goes for the Association."

And today.

I finally received confirmation from Lee Seol-ah that all preparations were complete.

"Thanks. You worked hard."

"What hard work... Then buy me a drink later."

"Didn't we just have wine together here last time?"

"Not here. Outside."

"Sure, sure."

Indeed, becoming friends with Korea's number one really makes everything go smoothly. If I had done this alone, it would have dragged on for years.

Anyway, after discussing some practical matters, we officially decided to launch Yu-seong Corporation's PMC business.

With our combined financial power, it's the largest scale ever.

Now we just need to announce our launch.

So we plastered advertisements absolutely everywhere.

A PMC business—a collaboration between the shadow hero who pulls Korea's strings from behind and an outright villain.

Finally, it has arrived in the Republic of Korea.

Even if we can salvage just a few ability users, that's something. Especially since there might be some unpolished gems among them.

This is just the first batch, and if they succeed, the second batch will be larger, and so on. And we can have the first batch train the second? That's how it becomes automated.

The birth of my direct organization, Mango Squad... no, Ego-squad. Though they won't know it themselves.

We've set a long recruitment period. I've cast the bait, and now I just need to reap the harvest later. Having gotten this off my chest, I can think about the rest after recruitment ends.
