"This... isn't what I intended..."

Or more precisely, I 'pretended' to mutter bitterly.

Not intended my ass. Of course I intended it.

Given Stardus's righteous personality, there's no way she'd tolerate sacrificing someone.

She'd sooner sacrifice herself.

Stardus. Shin Haru.

In the original work, Shin Haru endures countless hardships.

All sorts of bizarre villains do their utmost to drag Stardus down to hell in various ways.

In the process, Shin Haru's body and mind become tattered. Despite being more righteous than anyone, she suffers endlessly.

The first day I realized I'd entered this strange world, that's what I resolved.

To lessen Stardus's—Shin Haru's—suffering, even just a little.

But there's a slight problem with that.

True to being the protagonist of a hero story, Shin Haru grows stronger the more hardships she endures.

The more her mind deteriorates, the stronger her abilities become; the more her body suffers, the stronger her abilities become.

A character like a weed, growing stronger by overcoming adversity.

But the important thing is, if I decide to eliminate the villains in her place, the adversity gradually disappears—that's the problem.

Wait, she needs to suffer, but because of me she's not suffering?

If she doesn't face hardship, she won't grow stronger.

And if she doesn't grow stronger, there are quite a few problems.

As I mentioned before, the original manga that formed this world suffers increasingly severe power inflation as it progresses.

South Korea, which was clearly filled with moderately strong heroes and moderately strong villains, somehow becomes a story of villains growing absurdly powerful over time and Stardus becoming equally absurdly strong to match them.

And America. In this world, America is just five minutes from total chaos. The country with the world's number one count of S-Rank Heroes—of which South Korea has none—and also the most S-Rank Villains. A country where all sorts of incidents and accidents break out daily.

In the latter part of the story, divine beings just start appearing, Earth's destruction looms, chaos erupts—and even the S-Ranks crawling all over America can't stop Earth's destruction, but our Stardus does.

This got long-winded, but the conclusion is: Stardus needs to face hardship and power up, if only to prevent Earth's destruction.

And now, I'm the one who has to give her that hardship.

Rather than suffering from all sorts of civilian casualties, property damage, and unpredictable random villains, wouldn't it be better for Stardus to receive hardship from me—with a well-structured, systematic hardship curriculum providing dedicated support? Anyway, it'll definitely be better.

And the first item in my hardship gift set: stopping an oncoming train.

Yes, it might be hard. It might be hard, but our Haru will endure it. Go Stardus!!

And at that moment, Stardus collided with the train.

Kwa-aang— A tremendous roar that reached even here.

**[Kyaaaaaaaaaah!!!!]**

I'm sorry, Subin. Please hang in there just a little longer...

Clouds of dust billowing up thickly. Breaking through that dust, the train continued to run.

However, the train was definitely slowing down gradually.

Because someone was holding out in front of it.

Screeeeech— The train, making that sound as its speed decreased, finally came to a stop just barely in front of the five bound people. Yes! It stopped! Phew. I let out a secret *sigh* of relief.

But wait, Stardus's okay, right? I clenched my fists and stared at that spot. She endured, right? She must have. There's no way my Stardus would fall to something like this. Yeah, of course she endured.

I felt my throat *gulp* audibly without realizing it. An unprecedented situation. The dust slowly began to clear.

I started feeling anxious again. What if she died? Uh, that wouldn't be good.

A moment of terror.

"Yes!!!... I mean, not that! Oh! Oh... Oh! She endured. She endured it. Wow, she actually withstood that. Right. This wasn't exactly what I had planned, but... yes. Well, she solved this dilemma in her own way. Hmm. Yes."
